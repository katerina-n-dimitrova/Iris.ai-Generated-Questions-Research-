Does indexing 10 synthetic questions per chunk (Condition B) improve retrieval of gold evidence over directly indexing the original chunks (Condition A)? Vector-search only — cosine similarity in a local ChromaDB. No BM25 / sparse / hybrid / RRF.

Headline: No — under dense-vector-only retrieval, indexing 10 synthetic questions per chunk does not improve retrieval over indexing the original chunks on this pilot. The two conditions are statistically tied on the primary metric (Evidence Recall@5), with a small, occasionally-significant deficit for generated questions at k=3–5.

9 · Final report — the 20 questions

1. How were the 15 articles selected?
Query-first greedy selection (seed 42): shuffle non-null queries, accept a query iff its evidence articles fit a 15-article budget, then keep every query fully covered by the resulting closed set. Retrieval results were never used.
2. How many benchmark queries were fully covered?
84 eligible (inference 30 / temporal 29 / comparison 25); 420 excluded (needed an article outside the 15).
3. How many chunks / synthetic questions?
154 token-chunks (256/50/80), and 1540 synthetic questions (exactly 10/chunk).
4. Did generated-question retrieval outperform chunk-vector retrieval?
No. Tied on Evidence Recall@5 (Δ=-0.005, p=0.87); baseline slightly ahead at k=3–5.
5. At which k was the largest difference?
k=1 favours B marginally (Hit@1 +0.036, n.s.); k=4–5 favour A and reach significance (Hit@4/@5 p≈0.03).
6. Did generated questions improve Hit@k?
Only at k=1 (n.s.). At k≥3 baseline is higher; Hit@4/@5 significantly so.
7. Evidence Recall@k?
No — baseline ≥ generated at every k>1 (none significant).
8. All-Evidence Hit@k (multi-hop)?
Marginally higher for B at k=4/5 (+0.024, n.s.); lower at k=3/10. A wash.
9. Did generated questions find the first evidence chunk earlier?
No — mean first-relevant rank A=1.952 vs B=2.329 (baseline earlier).
10. Did they help retrieve ALL evidence for multi-hop questions?
Complete-evidence achieved rate A=0.405 vs B=0.369; among those achieved, B slightly earlier (rank 5.355 vs 5.647). Net: no.
11. Which question types improved / worsened most?
See per-type table: differences are small and within noise on this n.
12. How many queries improved / unchanged / worsened (Evidence Recall@5)?
16 improved, 51 unchanged, 17 harmed.
13. How often did the top synthetic question point to a non-gold chunk?
40 of 84 queries (48%).
14. How often did a lower-ranked synthetic question point to the correct chunk?
38 queries — i.e. the gold chunk was usually reachable but a distractor question outranked it.
15. Partial vs complete multi-hop retrieval (B)?
55 partial vs 20 complete (9 no-evidence).
16. Did retrieval differences change answer accuracy?
Slightly. EM A=0.786 vs B=0.738; F1 0.794 vs 0.754 — baseline modestly ahead.
17. Index-size increase?
10.0× more vectors (154 → 1540); store 29.987MB → 36.391MB.
18. Extra offline generation + embedding cost?
291.45s of LLM generation (gpt-4o-mini, ~$0.02) plus 7.32s to embed questions vs 11.79s for chunks.
19. Online latency difference?
Query embed 41.389ms (shared); search A 1.386ms vs B 3.805ms (B fetches 10× candidates + dedup).
20. Is the improvement worth ~10× more vectors?
No. There is no retrieval or answer-quality gain to justify the 10× index and the extra offline generation cost on this pilot.

Method & reproducibility

Both conditions share the same 15 articles, cleaning, token-chunk boundaries (256/50/80), eligible queries, embedding model, cosine metric, k values, gold mapping, answer LLM & prompt, and seed (42). The only difference is the indexed vector representation: one chunk vector (A) vs ten generated-question vectors mapped back to the parent chunk via max-similarity (B). Query vectors are transient and never stored. Gold evidence and gold answers were used only to build the closed collection and the evaluation labels — never during indexing, question generation, or retrieval scoring. Run with python run_vo.py.
