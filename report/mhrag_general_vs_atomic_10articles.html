Verdict: B — plain "10 general questions per chunk" — is the best of the three. It edges the chunk baseline on Evidence Recall@5 (0.542 vs 0.489, +0.053) and wins Precision@5 significantly (p=0.04), and it beats the atomic+chunk-level mix on almost every metric — Precision@5 by +0.075 (p<0.01) and Evidence Recall@5 by +0.068 (p=0.07). The extra machinery in E (atom decomposition + round-trip/margin filtering) did not help — simple general questions retrieved gold evidence more precisely. B trails only slightly on MRR@10 (first-hit rank), not significantly.

A = one vector per original chunk · B = 10 unrestricted questions/chunk (954 vectors) · E = pooled atomic-fact + chunk-level questions, filtered (676 vectors), mapped to parent by max similarity.

Closed-collection 10-article pilot (n=69) — not full-corpus MultiHop-RAG. Most single-metric deltas are directional (n is small); the consistent pattern (B ≥ A ≥/≈ E, B significantly more precise) is the signal. Baselines for A are identical across all comparisons by construction.
