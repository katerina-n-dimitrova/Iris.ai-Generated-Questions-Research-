DocBank · chunk size × generated-question enrichment

Does the enrichment needed depend on chunk size, and do bigger chunks want more questions? The PeerQA chunk-size study, replicated on DocBank layout text: 5 chunkings (four fixed sizes + one section-aware variable) × fixed and adaptive question counts.

Experimental setup

same 15 DocBank docs · reused synthetic QA · gold remapped by evidence

The 118 synthetic eval questions are reused as queries; each question's gold chunk is remapped onto every chunking by locating its verbatim evidence text. Only questions whose evidence maps in all 5 chunkings are kept — a common set of 88 queries, so sizes are directly comparable. Retrieval embeds ONLY generated questions (parent chunk returned on a hit); a chunk-text index is the baseline and a fused variant keeps the chunk vector.

  • Chunk sizes: 200 / 400 / 600 / 800 tokens (overlap ~22%) + a section-aware variable chunking (cap 800).
  • Embedder: huggingface:Octen/Octen-Embedding-0.6B:dim1024. LLM: gpt-4o-mini.
  • Question pool: up to 15 grounded questions/chunk (cached); q5/q10/q13/q15 and adaptive allocations are first-k slices.
  • Gold coverage per chunking: s200 88, s400 89, s600 89, s800 89, svar 89 of 118 (evidence-text match).

Analysis

chunk size vs question enrichment
  • Do larger chunks benefit from more questions? Yes — the best fixed count rises with size (200t→13q, 400t→13q, 600t→15q, 800t→15q). More questions help most where a chunk holds more distinct facts.
  • Is q10 a sweet spot across sizes? q10 is the top fixed count in 0/4 sizes; DocBank chunks are small/dense so the plateau often sits at 13, 13, 15, 15 questions respectively.
  • “Bigger chunk ⇒ more questions” (adaptive length) vs fixed q10: adaptive length wins (nDCG 0.664 vs 0.639) — and at avg 5.98 q/chunk (fewer than 10), so it is also cheaper.
  • Do smaller chunks need fewer questions? Yes — a 200-token DocBank chunk holds few facts, so the LLM makes fewer grounded questions and extra requested ones become near-duplicate embeddings; small chunks saturate earliest.
  • Do larger chunks get too broad/noisy? Larger chunks lift recall (the right chunk is easier to hit) but blur rank-1; more questions give a big chunk several sharp entry points, which is why bigger chunks gain most from more questions.
  • Gains from size, count, or both? Chunk size alone moves baseline nDCG@10 by ~0.042; question count within a size by up to ~0.116. Question count is the larger lever here, but they interact.
  • Worth the cost? Generated questions cost N× embeddings + a one-off LLM pass. On DocBank (synthetic eval) they clearly help; the smallest count on each size's plateau captures most of the gain — going to q15 rarely pays.
Caveat
The eval questions and enrichment questions are both LLM-generated from the same chunks, which flatters questions-only retrieval (as in the single-size DocBank run). Treat absolute levels as optimistic; the size×count shape is the finding.

Recommendation

one practical setup
  • Best fixed setup: chunk size 600 tok with 15 questions/chunk (nDCG@10 0.7103). Overall best across all conditions: chunk_size_600_q15 (0.7103).
  • Adaptive rule (“bigger ⇒ more”): adopt length-based allocation — it beats fixed q10 at lower cost (cap 15, floor ~4).
  • Length vs density: length-based is simpler and competitive; density needs only cheap regex signals (numbers, symbols, Table/Eq refs).
  • Precision: for rank-1-critical use keep the chunk vector and fuse with the question vectors instead of going questions-only.
  • Next: replace synthetic QA with real/citation-grounded eval to remove generator overlap, and sample table/figure-rich documents to stress layout retrieval.
